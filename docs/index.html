The last minute, in the menu.

Open the menu for a live chart of the past 60 seconds, average and peak draw, and recorded energy for the past minute, hour, day, week, month, and three months.

03

Energy & Apps.

A full dashboard with Overview, Applications, and History pages. Hover the chart for the power at any moment, and search or sort apps by CPU energy on supported Macs.

04

Up to 90 days of history.

Averages and energy totals build up while WattBar runs. Recent readings keep fine detail; older ones are stored as compact hourly summaries, on your Mac only.

Show power usage, a battery icon, a percentage, or a combination, with a live preview in Preferences. Battery controls appear only on Macs with a battery.

A FEW DETAILS

Good to know.

Small app. Straight answers.

Will it work on my Mac?+
WattBar runs on macOS 13 Ventura or later, with a universal download for Apple silicon and Intel. Power sensor availability differs by model. Battery features require an installed battery.
How do I install it?+

Download the ZIP, unzip it, and move WattBar.app to Applications.

Version 1.3.1 is ad-hoc signed and has not been notarized by Apple. macOS may block its first launch. Read the release notes before downloading.

What do the power numbers mean?+

System Total uses your Mac’s system power sensor. Battery shows the magnitude of the battery’s charge or discharge power. Where a battery sensor is unavailable, WattBar can calculate battery power from voltage and current.

When running on battery, that calculation can also estimate system power. It is not used as total system power while plugged in. These are hardware readings and estimates, not a wall-socket energy meter.

How much work does it do in the background?+
By default, WattBar reads sensors about every two seconds, with timer tolerance so macOS can batch work. You can choose one, two, five, or ten seconds. App CPU energy is sampled about every five seconds, or at your refresh rate if that is slower. Polling pauses during sleep, and history is saved about once a minute.
What does WattBar save?+

Up to 90 days of power and per-app energy history, stored in Application Support on your Mac. Nothing is uploaded. Averages use recorded time only: sleep, missing readings, and time before WattBar was running stay as gaps.

History from before you installed 1.3.0 can’t be recovered, so the longer periods fill in as WattBar runs.

Is app energy the same as Activity Monitor?+
No. WattBar shows each app’s CPU energy from macOS counters, or CPU time where those counters aren’t available. It doesn’t include display, GPU, network, or other device power, so it isn’t a breakdown of your whole Mac’s use, and it isn’t Activity Monitor’s Energy Impact score. How measurements work.
Can it launch at login?+
Yes. Turn on Launch at Login in Preferences. If macOS needs approval, WattBar links you to Login Items in System Settings.
How do updates work?+
WattBar uses Sparkle to check GitHub Releases. Choose Check for Updates from its menu, or control automatic checks in Preferences. Updates are verified with the existing WattBar signing key. Version 1.1.0 can update directly; older copies need one manual download.
